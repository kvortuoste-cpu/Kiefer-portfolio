Improving financial well-being through AI assistants

Helping Gen Z build financial literacy and confidence through personalized AI-driven tools for budgeting, education, and investment.

The Problem

Financial literacy is at an all-time low among adults, which directly impacts financial well-being. After initial research, findings showed Gen Z (aged 18–25) averaged 43% on a financial literacy test — the lowest scores among generations — but with the highest desire to improve their financial literacy and overall financial well-being.

43% Avg. financial literacy score for Gen Z
~20% Of the population is Gen Z
80% Want better financial understanding

With around 20% of the population being Gen Z, increasing financial literacy through education can help banks acquire and retain customers. Three main focus areas emerged: financial education, managing money, and go-to sources.

How might we help young adults (age 18–25) gain the knowledge to make smart financial decisions and increase their financial well-being?

Research

A survey was conducted to understand where Gen Z currently stands with their financial literacy. Key findings:

  • 40% learned about finances through their parents
  • 80% are dissatisfied or somewhat dissatisfied with their current level of financial literacy
  • 80% agree that they want a better overall understanding of finances
  • 40% want to learn through an advisor or personal course

Empathy Map synthesized from user interviews

A user interview was conducted to understand user behaviour toward managing finances. Findings were mapped onto an empathy map, revealing pain points around financial anxiety, distrust of generic advice, and a strong desire for personalized guidance.

Synthesizing design requirements from user interview

The Solution

A personal financial advisor assistant that helps with budgeting, personal and investment advice. Problem areas were mapped directly to design solutions:

  • Financial Education → Chatbots & financial testing
  • Managing Money → Financial assistants
  • Go-to Sources → Financial advice

Flowchart of the system

Features

Chatbots & Financial Testing

  • Allows users to ask questions related to a specific financial area
  • Allows users to ask any question related to finances
  • Bases tests off of knowledge rather than memory by generating unique test questions

Financial Assistants

  • Budget Assistant generates a unique budget plan based on the user's financial situation
  • Expense Assistant tracks expenses and generates a report to adjust spending habits
  • Investment Assistant manages the user's portfolio and gives predictions

Financial Advice

Getting financial advice can be accessed through chatbots or assistants, which all cross-reference each other to give holistic and specific financial guidance.

Technical Requirements

Four core technical requirements were defined:

  • Conversational AI for Chatbot
  • Generate data visualization and graphs
  • Generate questions for financial testing
  • Access to third-party banking platforms

For chatbot and test question generation, an LLM like ChatGPT or Cohere gives the best flexibility for scalability and security over financial information. Data visualizations can be generated using a third-party service like Chartify or ChartAI.

Technical map for different app features

Limitations & Considerations

Limitations

  • Contextual Advice — AI can be trained to give financial advice, but also needs to learn about user end goals, attitudes, behaviours, and their financial situation to give truly useful guidance.
  • Prompting the System — Prompt engineering is relatively new. Since it's the user generating the prompt, there's an assumption the user already knows the problem, as opposed to human advisors who can ask questions to diagnose it.

Considerations

  • Gaining Trust — Trust is at the heart of every financial decision. The product needs to be designed so that users can trust the AI with their financial livelihood.
  • Financial Nuance — Large financial decisions are situational and nuanced. There's rarely any black and white.
  • Learning through Generative AI — Understanding how people learn and the impact of AI on learning is something that needs further research.
  • Premature for Gen Z — People in Gen Z are still young and might not be in financial situations where they need to think about these topics.

Reflection

This project taught me how to design and technically implement an AI-based product. It deepened my understanding of how to balance user trust, technical feasibility, and genuine educational value within a single product. Next steps would be to conduct usability testing and iterate based on user feedback.
