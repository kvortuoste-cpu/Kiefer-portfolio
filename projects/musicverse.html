Redefining how artists grow and engage with fans

An NFT marketplace that enables artists to sell song ownership directly to fans — giving artists instant revenue and fans exclusive access.

The Problem

Musicians who rely on streaming services as their source of income struggle to make ends meet. Artists make money either through touring, merch sales, or album sales and streaming. Since streaming has become the norm for music consumers, smaller artists that don't have an established following to tour or sell merch are left behind.

~$0.003 Per stream on average for artists
70%+ Revenue taken by middlemen
2 Key personas: artists & fans

Research

Through secondary research and user interviews with a music artist and a music consumer, a disconnect was found between the artist and the consumer. Key findings:

  • Most of the revenue made from artists goes to middlemen — streaming platforms, music labels, etc.
  • Artists need to develop a deeper connection with their fans
  • Fans want to increase engagement with their favourite artists
  • Fans feel responsible for an artist's success

The Solution

An NFT marketplace that enables artists to partially sell their song ownership directly to fans, who get access to exclusive benefits. NFTs are leveraged to verify ownership of a digital song and grant access to owner benefits.

Artists partially sell their songs and receive revenue instantly. Fans get direct access to the artist — exclusive content, experiences, and a genuine stake in the music they love.

The final solution enables artists to partially sell their songs and be given access to revenue instantly, while fans are given direct access to the artist. This model cuts out middlemen entirely — the value flows directly between creator and fan.

Reflection

This project helped me deeply understand blockchain, NFTs, and crypto as technologies — and more importantly, how to design a practical, human-centered use for them. Since the technology is uncharted, it enabled trail-blazing a new experience for emerging tech, forcing me to think about trust, onboarding complexity, and education as core design challenges rather than afterthoughts.
